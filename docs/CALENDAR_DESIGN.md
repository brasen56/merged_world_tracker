## 1. Goal

Models handle Gregorian dates because they memorized them. A fantasy calendar has no prior at all: month names, month lengths, weekday cycles, and which month is winter are invented per world, so the narrator invents them per turn. Concretely, today:

- A tag with a fantasy month fails closed by design (`parseAmbientDateTime` knows twelve English months or a renamed twelve; unknown names return `null`), so the ladder falls to World State or message-count — and the moon, tides, and seasonal crossings silently vanish from that chat (D26/D35).
- A user who hand-edits the preset to emit custom tags gets no validation: month lengths drift turn over turn, weekdays drift worse than Gregorian (Phase 7 exists because even Gregorian drifts), and "snow on the first of Highsun" has no guard.
- "What date are we walking toward" (v2 §1) has no home: festival dates are pasted by hand and go stale — v2's own injection example ends "The Lantern Festival is in three days", but nothing computes that sentence. And ordinary Gregorian slice-of-life stories want "the wedding is in five days" just as much (D49).

Same fix as v1 and v2: **code computes the date facts, the model renders them.** A calendar is the one ambient input that is pure structure — months, lengths, weekdays, one anchor — and a calendar anchored once is just a second formatting of story hours. Every computed channel already shipped (sun, season, moon, tides, crossings, phenomena) keeps working unchanged; this module adds the formatting, the agreement, and the authored facts on top.

Three jobs, in order: **state today's date right** (custom tags, a stated date line, drift detection), **make sky and season agree with the calendar by construction** (the midsummer anchor), and **host user-authored world facts on dates** (events with repeats, date-shaped phenomena). Explicitly not a job: advancing time. The clock advances only as narrated, through tags — D3/D6 unchanged. The calendar formats and validates, never invents.

The audience is the same slice-of-life / slow-burn RP as v1/v2, plus the fantasy-settings subset of it: the story that says "the third of Rotmonth" and means it.

## 2. Decisions this design adopts

| # | Decision |
|---|----------|
| D38 | **A custom calendar is a pure day-number map with one authored anchor** — the moon-anchor pattern (D25) applied to dates. The record lives at `mwt_calendar` (chat-local, user-authored, ambient-independent, inherited by branches via metadata copy, like the ambient seed D2). Everything computed — a date's story hours, its weekday, its season, the sun above it — is a pure function of (record, story hours); nothing is persisted on evaluation, nothing can desync across swipes, branches, or rewinds. **A chat without `mwt_calendar` behaves exactly as today** — Gregorian stays the default calendar and every existing test must pass unmodified (the zero-regression gate, Phases 11–12). |
| D39 | **Structure:** months with names and lengths (1–24 months, each 1–40 days), an optional weekday list (2–14 names, or none for weekday-less calendars), an optional era label. Days are 24 h and time-of-day is unchanged (HH:MM). Years are plain integers; the era is a display prefix, never parsed. Names: 1–24 characters, **letters in any language**, spaces, apostrophes, hyphens (no digits, commas, brackets, or angle brackets — the phenomena-clause injection-safety rule), unique case-insensitively within their list; **curly apostrophes are normalized to straight ones** on save and on parse, so a typographic difference can never make a tag unreadable. **No leap rules, no intercalary/epagomenal days, no multi-era chains** — fixed month lengths keep the map pure and the arithmetic integer (the `'gregorian'` structure gets the shipped leap calendar instead, D49). **Edits preserve identity:** inserting or reordering a month renumbers every reference to it (events, schedules, the anchor) with its month; shortening a month that orphans a referenced day offers clamping to that month's new last day — explicitly, never silently. |
| D40 | **The midsummer anchor:** one authored world fact — "midsummer falls on \<date\> (and it is a \<weekday\>)" — defines the day-count origin. **The anchor year defaults to the story's current year** at authoring (when a dated clock resolves; otherwise the user supplies it and the preview appears once a clock exists), because drift counts from the anchor year: a 354-day calendar anchored 7 years before the story puts "midsummer" in spring, 47 years puts it in winter. **No solstice is stored** — the origin derives at read from the anchor date and the ambient hemisphere (south → the December reference, north or unset → June), so the fact is never stored twice (D18) and a later hemisphere change or an imported calendar cannot strand midsummer on the wrong solstice; a hemisphere flip repositions the calendar coherently (every custom date shifts together; hours-stored settings are D48's business). The panel previews the derived season and solstice **for today's date** — catching anchor-year drift and hemisphere mismatches in one view that the anchor-date preview (summer by construction) never could. Declination, `seasonAt`, tides, moon, and crossings all read story hours today; the anchor guarantees calendar-summer = sun-summer = weather-baseline-summer **by construction, with zero changes to any shipped formula** (the response §5.6). |
| D41 | **Non-365-day years drift against the sun, and that is accepted.** The solar model stays the shipped one — Gregorian, **leap years included** (a 365.2425-day average), so drift per in-story year is Y − 365.2425 days: a 354-day lunar year ~11¼ days (the real Islamic-calendar property; spring after 7 years, winter after 47), a 360-day year ~5¼, a 366-day year ~¾ late, and even a 365-day calendar drifts one day per ~4 years. This is the world's own fact if its orbit is Earth-like, it matches the response's accepted trade ("drift only shows up over several in-story years"), and no stretching of the solar year, per-year re-anchoring, or drift warning is added. The today-preview (D40) makes any of it visible whenever it matters. |
| D42 | **The tag grammar generalizes through the calendar record — and when a `custom` record exists, only the custom grammar is accepted.** `[In-story time: HH:MM (AM/PM), <Weekday>, <Month> D, YYYY]` with the record's names, the weekday segment optional (omitted for weekday-less calendars). A Gregorian-format tag or scene date is **ignored, never parsed as a real date**: one slip like "June 3, 1247" would otherwise move the story ~753 years and the date line would repeat it as authoritative; the Overview says why the tag was passed over (with a diagnostics event, the Phase 7 pattern). The parser fails closed on unknown month names exactly as today (no wrong date, ever), accepts month names with spaces, **tolerates the record's era label after the year** (an era-bearing tag is never unreadable), and treats the weekday slot as a free token validated against the list afterward — an **unknown weekday is a drift mismatch (D43), not an unreadable date**. The v1 `monthNames` seam (twelve renamed Gregorian months) stays as-is for compatibility and applies only to chats without a record. The era never appears in the canonical tag. |
| D43 | **Weekday drift generalizes to custom lists:** the computed weekday is the day count modulo the list length from the anchor's weekday; the tagged-vs-computed comparison, the Overview badge, and the diagnostics event keep their Phase 7 mechanics with custom names. An unknown weekday token in the tag counts as a mismatch (it is wrong by definition — not in this calendar) and badges accordingly; weekday-less tags have nothing to compare and stay silent. The badge is the ceiling of loudness: **no injection copy ever warns about drift** (D33 discipline); the date line (D44) is the preventive tool, since stating tomorrow's full date removes the arithmetic the model gets wrong. |
| D44 | **The calendar speaks through its own gated block, not ambient's.** One `<mwt_calendar>` injection via the standard seam (`applyExtensionPromptInjection`, budget-managed, manifest interceptor timing per D3 so swipes resolve to the same clock). The **date line** requires: a `custom`-structure record, a dated clock, **a newest tag that parsed** — a newest tag that failed turns the whole block off with the reason on the Overview (a stale date stated as authoritative pushes the model to roll the story back; custom names will fail more often than English ones) — and the per-chat toggle. The line states **today and tomorrow with weekday names** plus a **month-arithmetic clause shown only when 7 or fewer days remain in the month** ("5 days remain in Rotmonth; the next month is Harvest" — the year number too at year end): tomorrow already covers the one-day skips, the clause covers the week-scale ones, and everyday defaults earn no tokens every turn (D33); months shorter than 7 days always show it, which is right — that is where the model overruns the month end most. This is the arithmetic a time skip needs, replacing ambient's "keep it vague" guard, which fits weather but contradicts a preset's mandatory timestamp tag. **The generated preset snippet carries the full month table** (names and day counts, in order) — fixed text with no per-turn cost that covers longer skips and stays available when a bad tag switches the block off, so the model can recover instead of writing Rotmonth 38, 39, 40. **The era is omitted from the line** (it would teach the model to tag it) though the parser tolerates it (D42). An **events line** (D45) rides the same gates. Ambient's injection body gains nothing from the calendar — the revision 4 token audit (D33) stays valid — and the new block carries its own budget, measured with the same tokenizer harness. The overrun is accepted on the revision-4 argument: a custom calendar is an explicit per-chat choice. |
| D45 | **Events are user-authored world facts** (the third kind of line, v2 §5), stored at `mwt_calendar.events`, **on either structure** (D49): stable ID, name, a calendar date (leap-validated where the structure is Gregorian), an optional duration (1–14 days), and a repeat — none, yearly, every N years (2–99), or **lunar** (new/full), computed from the anchored moon (the Phase 6 seam; a re-anchored moon moves lunar events with it, ±1 day near midnight per D23's scatter, documented). Injection obeys **D32 unchanged**: at most the next two upcoming events, only within ~14 days, a second only if both are within a week; ongoing events state their remaining days; no forecast beyond that, no "fired" state — distances are recomputed pure, so rewinding restores "in three days". The panel lists all events regardless of lookahead; planner context gains the same bounded line (parity, D15 pattern). Cancellation is a user edit, never detected or performed by the module. |
| D46 | **Dated phenomena are the second shape in the shipped phenomena engine.** Schedules live at `mwt_calendar.phenomena` (date-shaped rules are meaningless without the calendar; editing structure rewrites their meaning, so they live where that trade is visible): ID/name/clause caps identical to frequency rules, a start date, a duration in days (1–400) or calendar months (1–13), an optional yearly repeat, and an optional end. **Inclusive start, exclusive end; End now stores the current story instant as a calendar date+time, not hours**, so re-anchoring carries it with the calendar. Evaluated pure from the resolved clock; no start gates (schedules don't roll); **visibility gates are supported** (the existing regime/sky/wind/season schema — so "a dim eclipse veils the sun" need not show at midnight). **Light overrides only darken, in either shape:** the effective regime is the darker of the natural regime and the authored value — a month-long eclipse turns noon into twilight but never lifts 2 AM out of darkness, and moonlight reading the effective light is unaffected at night (a brightening "fixed" mode for endless-day world rules exists only if a story demands it, §8.9). **Light composes by darkest-wins across both shapes:** the darkest active override wins outright — integer priority −100..100 and ascending rule ID only pick whose name is shown on a tie — so a priority-0 darkness rule beats a priority-10 twilight one and nothing can make a scene lighter; at most three visible clauses total across shapes, omitted count reported. This composition **applies to the shipped frequency engine as of revision 3** (landed on `testing` before the 2.14.0 merge, with `AMBIENT_PHENOMENA.md` and the changelog amended and one test changed deliberately — revision 3 note); `daylight` remains an accepted value with no effect, because rejecting it would silently drop stored rules. A message-count clock leaves dated rules inactive with an Overview-only unavailable notice (revision 4 parity: notices never enter injection bodies). Editing a schedule rewrites its evaluated past and future (the D30 trade, stated in the UI); **Delete erases history and the UI says so**. |
| D47 | **Layout follows the family rules.** Pure math in `core/calendar_math.js` (validation, day-index↔hours maps for both structures, weekday, formatting, anchor derivation, bounded event projection, date-interval evaluation); the calendar seam enters `core/ambient_time.js` as an optional parameter; `core/weekday_drift.js` generalizes in place; the dated shape enters `core/ambient_phenomena.js` consuming `calendar_math`. UI in a `calendar/` module folder (the `ambient/` pattern), joining the manifest interceptor chain; a Calendar tab in the main modal's tab family plus Overview cells. **The Ambient panel's dated inputs — the moon-anchor picker and pin "until date" — accept and express custom dates when a record exists** (stored values stay hours; the UX parses and formats through the calendar), since lunar events depend on the moon anchor being settable in story terms. No module-to-module imports (D15): ambient and planner consume the calendar only through the shared core projection (`readAmbientConditions` passes the `mwt_calendar` record into the resolver). |
| D48 | **Adopting a calendar is a clock-domain switch, and stored times never silently pretend otherwise.** Pins and frequency phenomena store absolute story hours plus the clock domain they were created under; adopting a calendar moves a message-count chat onto the dated domain, where message-count pins and rules go inactive by their shipped source guards (`core/ambient_phenomena.js:64`) — the panel says so at adoption and **offers re-stamping**: dated values re-map by calendar date, and hours-stored starts/bounds that have no date meaning under the old axis reset to the current story time. Re-anchoring, structure edits, and hemisphere flips (D40's repositioning) get the same offer. Declining leaves the old values frozen on the hours axis — a stated trade, never a silent one. Date-referenced records (events, dated schedules, dated ends) need no re-stamping: they follow the calendar by construction. |
| D49 | **`structure: 'gregorian' \| 'custom'`** (default `'custom'`): ordinary Gregorian chats get events and dated phenomena without adopting fantasy months, so a modern slice-of-life story can have "the wedding is in five days". A `'gregorian'` record carries only version/structure, the toggles, events, and phenomena — months, weekday lists, and anchor are absent, the shipped Gregorian grammar and leap-year arithmetic apply unchanged, and Phase 7 weekday drift covers it as today. Record dates are Gregorian {year, month, day}, leap-validated. Custom-only tag rejection (D42) applies only to `'custom'` records. The date line remains a custom-calendar tool (§8.5): `'gregorian'` chats keep badge-only drift handling. Recreating Gregorian as custom months is not supported — fixed lengths have no leap days — which is exactly why the field exists. |

## 3. Target contract

### 3.1 The calendar record

Chat-local `mwt_calendar`, flat like `mwt_ambient`:

```json
{
    "version": 1,
    "structure": "custom",
    "months": [
        { "name": "Snowmoon", "days": 36 }, { "name": "Icewind", "days": 36 },
        { "name": "Thawmoon", "days": 36 }, { "name": "Seedmoon", "days": 36 },
        { "name": "Bloom", "days": 36 }, { "name": "Highsun", "days": 36 },
        { "name": "Rotmonth", "days": 36 }, { "name": "Harvest", "days": 36 },
        { "name": "Frostmoon", "days": 36 }, { "name": "Deepnight", "days": 36 }
    ],
    "weekdays": ["Starday", "Oneday", "Twoday", "Threeday", "Fiveday", "Sixday"],
    "era": "Kelen Epoch",
    "anchor": { "year": 1247, "month": 6, "day": 18, "weekday": 3 },
    "dateLine": true,
    "eventsLine": true,
    "events": [],
    "phenomena": []
}
```

A 10-month, 360-day year (drifts ~5¼ days per in-story year against the leap-aware sun, D41); the anchor puts midsummer in **Highsun** (month 6). A `'gregorian'` record is `{ version, structure: "gregorian", eventsLine, events, phenomena }`.
- `weekdays: null` is a weekday-less calendar (tags omit the weekday; no drift check). `era: null` omits the era everywhere.
- Validation (D39 bounds) is pure and total; the anchor date must exist in the structure and its weekday index must be in range. Authoring years are 1–9999; computed dates before year 1 format as bare numbers, diagnostics-only, never a crash.
- The record is the whole state. Deleting it returns the chat to Gregorian exactly as today; export/import is a JSON clipboard round-trip (the chronicle `import-export` pattern) — no account-level library (§7). Adoption, re-anchoring, and structure edits surface the D48 consequences before saving.

### 3.2 Anchoring

With months of lengths `L₁…Lₖ`, year length `Y = ΣLᵢ`, and the anchor `{year, month, day, weekday}`:

- `dayIndex(y, m, d) = (y − anchor.year)·Y + Σ_{i<m} Lᵢ + (d − 1)` — an integer, negative before the anchor (floored arithmetic throughout, D23's spirit). The `'gregorian'` structure's day index is days since 1970-01-01 by UTC-shaped arithmetic (leap years included).
- `originHours = SOLSTICE_HOURS(hemisphere) − dayIndex(anchor)·24`, **derived at read**: the December reference when ambient's hemisphere is `south`, the June reference otherwise (unset ambient defaults June — invisible without astronomy, and a later hemisphere flip repositions coherently, D40). The reference instants are the UTC-shaped midnights of 2000-06-21 and 2000-12-21; the reference year is an internal constant, never surfaced as story truth. The pin is definitional, like the moon epoch (D19's accuracy stance: stated against our own formula, not an almanac).
- `hours(date, hh:mm) = originHours + dayIndex·24 + hh + mm/60`; the inverse is `floor((hours − originHours)/24)` plus a month walk, negative-safe.
- `weekday(dayIndex) = weekdays[flooredMod(anchor.weekday + dayIndex − dayIndex(anchor), n)]`.

**Agreement by construction:** every shipped channel reads story hours — declination from Gregorian day-of-year (`ambient_astronomy.js:190`), `seasonAt` from Gregorian months with the hemisphere shift (`ambient_generator.js:93`), tides and moon from hours directly. The anchor places the custom midsummer date (of the anchor year) on the reference solstice, so calendar-summer, sun-summer (declination ≈ +23.44°), and weather-summer agree for both hemispheres with **no change to any of those formulas**. The weather baseline peaks about three and a half weeks after the anchor date (mid-July vs June 21 — v1's deliberate lag, temperatures trail the sun); that is a property of the weather model, not a disagreement. Drift in later years is D41's accepted trade, made visible by the today-preview. Re-anchoring or editing the structure rewrites the calendar's past and future consistently (the moon-anchor property); what that means for stored times is D48, not a silent migration.

### 3.3 Time pipeline

- When a `custom` record exists, tags and World State scene dates parse through the custom grammar **only**; Gregorian-format input is passed over with an Overview note and a diagnostics event. Unknown month names fail closed to the ladder; curly apostrophes are normalized before matching; an era suffix after the year is tolerated; the weekday slot is a free token checked against the list (unknown → drift mismatch, D43). A chat that adopts a calendar keeps running on its old Gregorian tags' fallbacks until new custom tags exist — the adoption flow says so (D48).
- The resolver carries the formatted custom date, the grammar used, and — when the newest tag failed — the rejection (message index and reason) so surfaces can explain a missing date line (D44).
- Weekday drift (D43): tag vs `weekday(dayIndex)`; badge + event; weekday-less silent.
- Ambient consumes the resolved hours exactly as today — `readAmbientConditions` passes the record into the resolver, and every downstream channel (daylight, moon, tide, aftermath, phenomena) is unchanged code. The motivating fantasy scenario is the Phase 11 exit: a 60°N story on a custom calendar reads bright at 21:00 in Highsun and dark at 15:00 in Deepnight, with the moon and tides back online.

### 3.4 The calendar block (date line + events line)

Draft copy (final wording constant-swappable and tokenizer-measured at Phase 12), coherent with the §3.1 example:

```
<mwt_calendar>
Stage direction, not narration. In-world date (authoritative): Fiveday, Rotmonth 31, 1247;
tomorrow is Sixday, Rotmonth 32. 5 days remain in Rotmonth; the next month is Harvest.
The Lantern Festival begins in three days. Advance the date only as in-story time
advances, using the dates stated here.
</mwt_calendar>
```

- Gated channels, D33 style: no block without a `custom` record and a dated clock; **no block when the newest tag failed to parse** (the Overview states the reason — misspelling, day 31 of a 30-day month, and so on); no events line without an in-window event; each line independently toggleable.
- Today and tomorrow with weekdays, plus the month-arithmetic clause **only when 7 or fewer days remain in the month** (remaining days, next month's name, the year number at year end) — one day of lookahead always, the week-scale material near month end: the D8 shape applied to dates. No era in the line (D42/D44).
- `/wt-calendar` returns the body verbatim (parity with `/wt-ambient`). No mutating subcommands; edits happen in the panel.

### 3.5 Events

```json
{ "id": "lantern-festival", "name": "The Lantern Festival",
  "date": { "year": 1247, "month": 7, "day": 34 }, "durationDays": 2, "repeat": "yearly" }
```

- Works on both structures (D49); `'gregorian'` dates are leap-validated (February 29 legal in leap years).
- Repeats: `"none"`, `"yearly"` (the month/day recurs; with fixed lengths every date exists every year), `{"everyYears": n}` (2–99), or `{"lunar": "new" | "full"}` (no stored date; the next phase instant from the anchored moon names the event day).
- Bounded lookahead (D45/D32): next event within ~14 days; a second only if both are within a week; an ongoing event states remaining days ("the second and final day of the Lantern Festival"); at most two event mentions total.
- Boundary (v2 §5): user-authored world facts. The story may narrate around a festival; only the user edits or deletes the record. Ordinary appointments need no ambient effect and get none — events carry no weather or sky consequence; only phenomena (D46) do.

### 3.6 Dated phenomena (the deferred half of §3.5.1/D37)

```json
{
    "id": "rot-eclipse", "shape": "dated", "name": "The Rotmonth eclipse",
    "clause": "a dim eclipse veils the sun",
    "start": { "year": 1247, "month": 7, "day": 3 },
    "durationMonths": 1, "repeat": "yearly",
    "end": null, "light": "twilight", "priority": 10,
    "visibilityGates": { "regime": ["daylight", "twilight"] }
}
```

- Active iff the end (when set) is ahead and `hours` lies in `[occurrenceStart, occurrenceStart + duration)` for some occurrence — starts inclusive at midnight, ends exclusive; occurrences at the start date and (yearly) each year's same date. Durations are days (1–400) or calendar months (1–13) of the record's own lengths — "one month" means the calendar's month.
- **Light only darkens (D46):** effective regime = the darker of natural and authored, ordered darkness < twilight < daylight. The eclipse above turns noon into twilight; at 2 AM the scene stays darkness and moonlight descriptions survive. Its visibility gates hide the clause at night. `end` is `null` or the calendar instant written by **End now** (exclusive) — the past survives, rewinding before it restores the phenomenon.
- Composition (both shapes, one engine, §3.6/D46): the darkest active light override wins outright — priority and ID only name the winner on a tie — with each override clamped by darker-of, aurora re-gated on the effective regime, and brightness/meteor visibility reading the effective light; at most three visible clauses total, omitted count reported. The frequency engine already composes this way (revision 3); moon phase and tides are untouched — a month-long authored eclipse is a world rule, not orbital mechanics (D34 unchanged).
- Clockless chats: dated rules inactive, Overview notice only. Deleting is the history-erasing option and the panel labels it so. Schedule edits and re-anchors follow D48's re-stamp offer.

## 4. Code layout

- `core/calendar_math.js` — pure: record validation (both structures), day-index↔hours maps, weekday, formatting, anchor derivation (hemisphere-aware), bounded event projection, date-interval evaluation. ## 5. Boundary notes (extends v2 §5)

Three kinds of line, one rule. **Computed ambient** (weather, sky, now dates): code decides, model renders. **Incidents**: the story's, World Pressures' — a festival raided, a bridge closed at spring tide. **User-authored world facts**: events and phenomenon schedules — the user writes them, the module renders their consequences, only the user edits or ends them. Ending a phenomenon at the story's dispersal moment (End now) keeps the played past intact; cancelling a festival is a record edit, not a detection. Calendar re-anchoring and structure edits rewrite the calendar's past and future consistently — the D30 trade stated in the UI at edit time, with D48's re-stamp offer for stored times.
